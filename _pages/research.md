## Ongoing Projects

### Dam removal reveals influence of sediment availability on transport dynamics
*PhD Project*

The Elwha River's dams were removed, starting in 2011. The US Geological Survey collected extensive suspended load data for five years following the removal. This project expands upon [MaGirl et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomorph.2014.12.032), and uses the USGS dataset ([Ritchie et al., 2018](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS:5a25e9f2e4b03852baf67f06)) to better understand suspended sediment dynamics. This research has been advised by Dr. Colin Phillips.

### Place-based learning of storm hydrology to increase Native student affinity toward geoscience
*MSc Project*

Under-representation of ethnic minorities in higher education geoscience is an ongoing issue and is most severe with Native students. I have created a hands-on, place-based exercise to work with Native students through USU's Native American Summer Mentorship Program and the Upward Bound Program at the USU Blanding Campus. This exercise uses a rainfall simulator to teach hillslope hydrology so students can understand dryland infiltration-runoff processes and how it applies to their local communities. This research was completed for my master's thesis, advised by Dr. Joel Pederson.

### Connecting Evapotranspiration, Water Supply, and Communities in Southern Colorado
*MSc Project*

The turn of the Millennium Drought has caused decreased snow pack across the western United States. As a result, there's been a decrease in water supply. This is noticeable in the Rio Grande River headwaters near San Luis Valley, Colorado. My team compared trends in evapotranspiration to meteorological variables and surface water supply to better understand how the San Luis Valley community can adapt to evolving conditions. This research is completed through USU's [Climate Adaptation Science Program](https://www.usu.edu/climate-adaptation/).

### Using OSL to quantify Colorado River incision near Dewey Bridge, UT
*MSc Project*

Reasons behind Colorado River incision have raised questions about how the river has responded to climate and tectonics. To quantify Colorado River incision near Dewey Bridge, I have collected OSL samples and began processing them at USU's Luminescence Lab. This is part of a larger project by USU PhD student Natalie Tanski, and complements previous work completed by USU MSc student Andrew Jochems.

### Watershed Modeling to Predict the Hydrological Impacts of Climate and Land Use
*BS Project*

Persisting drought across the Navajo Nation has led to questions about how water availability will change as climate change continues and subsequent land use change occurs. To investigate how water availability will respond to climate and land use, I used a physically-based watershed model for Black Rock Canyon within Canyon de Chelly National Monument. This research was used for my undergraduate research thesis at Northern Arizona University, mentored by Dr. Taylor Joyal, in conjunction with NAU MS student Natalia Shaw's thesis.
